Welcome to the Pat Summitt Definite Dozen Leadership Program!

If you aspire to be an effective leader, you may have pondered the question, "Are we born to be leaders, or can we develop the capacities we need in our life experience to lead effectively?" Coach Pat Summitt, the namesake for this training, believed leadership and the peak performance skills required to lead effectively could be developed and continuously improved.

In this online learning experience, we will unpack Coach Summitt's Definite Dozen — the guiding principles that she saw as fundamental to leadership excellence. We are excited to have you join us on this journey!

Meet Pat Summitt: Introduction Featuring Peyton Manning 
 
Peyton Manning, 2021 Pro Football Hall of Fame Inductee & Super Bowl Champion 
Hi, everyone. Peyton Manning, here. I’m thrilled that all of you are here today to hear the 
words of wisdom put forth by my dear friend, Pat Summitt. Pat was not just an iconic basketball 
coach. She was a trailblazer for women, a champion for gender equality, and an amazing 
educator who graduated 100 percent of her student-athletes at the University of Tennessee.  
Pat was clearly an American treasure. She used to say, ‘Nobody cares how much you know until 
they know how much you care.’ Pat’s caring attitude motivated her to create a formula for 
success and leadership in life called the Definite Dozen. These principles were the foundation 
of her programs for decades. 
 
Narrator 
Pat Summitt was a leadership and coaching superstar. Her stats alone are the stuff of legend. 
1,098 wins, 16 SEC titles, eight national championships, eight-time SEC coach of the year, 
seven-time NCAA coach of the year, Naismith coach of the century in 2000, and one of The 
Sporting News’ 50 greatest coaches of all time.  
Pat Summitt’s career was nothing short of spectacular, but her stats, as extraordinary as they 
are, only tell part of the story. Pat Summitt’s influence extends beyond the court to the lives 
she impacted and the lessons she left behind for achieving greatness.  
This is the story of a farm girl who fell in love with basketball and grew up to change the world 
of sports forever. Pat’s coaching tree continues to make an impact on the game today. Her 
influence has left an indelible impression on the entire sporting world.  
Pat Summitt always demanded the best from others and held herself to the same standard. 
When she was diagnosed with early onset dementia Alzheimer’s type in 2011, Pat faced it head 
on and decided to keep coaching. She stayed in the public eye while dealing with a crippling 
disease and displayed the sort of courage and resilience that mark a true leader.  
Those who tuned in for what many assumed would be Pat’s last season saw a woman bolstered 
by the tremendous support from the community she had cultivated at Tennessee. True to form, 
Coach Summitt kept on fighting and led her team to their 16th SEC championship. It would be 
the last time she performed the champion’s ritual of cutting down the basketball after the 
game.  
A few weeks later, Pat Summitt decided it was time to move on from the great passion of her 
life. In 2012, she retired from her position as head coach of the Lady Vols and was named head 

coach emeritus. That same year, she was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom and the 
Arthur Ashe Courage Award.  
Even as she stepped away from the spotlight Pat never admitted defeat. Instead, she shifted 
her focus to the fight against Alzheimer’s. Shortly after her diagnosis, Pat and her son Tyler 
created the Pat Summitt Foundation, an organization for awarding grants to nonprofits 
dedicated to advancing research into treatment and a cure for Alzheimer’s. Pat Summitt was in 
attendance when in January 2015 the University of Tennessee Medical Center and the Pat 
Summitt Foundation announced a partnership to build the Pat Summitt Clinic for furthering 
treatment capabilities, research initiatives, and family support for Alzheimer’s and other 
dementias. Sadly, Pat would not live to see the clinic’s opening in 2017. She passed away at the 
age of 64 on June 28, 2016.  
Shortly after her death, the Pat Summitt Leadership Group was formed to spread the words and 
teachings of Pat Summitt, using film, educational programs, and publishing, the Pat Summitt 
Leadership Group uses the core principles of Pat’s Definite Dozen to help leaders of all ages 
find personal and professional success.  
Pat’s influence lives on with every life that continues to be impacted by the enduring wisdom of 
her leadership philosophy and practice. Pat Summitt was more than a coach. She was a role 
model, a lifelong teacher, and a sterling example of courage and leadership.  
Her legacy is that of being the most successful coach in the history of women’s basketball, but 
she lived a life that goes beyond stellar statistics. It’s a life filled with lessons for what it looks 
like to win with people and leave the world a better place because of it.

## dd_mp_01_st_003 — Pat Summitt’s Gift for Leaders Who Follow

Type: text · Source sequence: 3

Pat Summitt’s Gift for Leaders Who Follow

Great leaders can be studied, and their principles and approaches emulated. In Pat Summitt’s case, decades of anecdotes from players and assistant coaches could give us at least a working understanding of what she did as a leader to become the most effective and successful college basketball coach of all time. However, Coach Summitt went one step further. She came up with a formula for outstanding leadership and peak performance. 

This formula, explored and applied in this learning experience, provides a very direct pathway to success that leaders and ascending performers can follow to more consistently and predictably achieve their goals. She called it The Definite Dozen, and it represents 12 critical habits and commitments that form the foundation of success for any leader or ascending performer who wants to improve and put themselves and those they lead in a much better position to win!
