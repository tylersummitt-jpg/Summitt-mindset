Defining leadership self-respect

Leadership self-respect includes both the level of respect leaders have for themselves, and the level of respect teammates observe the leader has for themselves.  

For example, a leader who shows up looking professional, in a good state of health, rested and ready, sends the message to the team that they care enough about themselves to bring the best version of who they are to the team. Then, the team will often respond well to the leader, and they become more likely to respect themselves in the same way.  

This creates a success cycle that builds momentum and begins to improve results.

Coach Pat Summitt 
My mom taught me a lot about respect. She only went through the ninth grade … loving, caring, 
respectful of everyone. The only person in my life I’ve never heard anyone say a bad word

My secretary has been with me (for) 23 years. Now that’s a long time to be with anyone. My

But Katie is the person that I talk to every morning when I get up. And when I started out 
working with Katie, I didn’t know Katie. I might call her and say, ‘Katie, look. Tell me the four 
things I have to do this morning.’ I quickly learned to say, ‘Katie’ … on a Monday morning … 
‘how was your weekend? How are you doing? How’s your family doing?’ And start to develop 
(the) relationship.  
My student managers … when you do what we do, we just recruit a lot of people to come help 
us. And our student managers are really, really key. We got ready this year to go to the Final 
Four. I turned in my list of who on our staff would be going to the Final Four.  
And Joan Cronan, our AD (athletic director), called me up and she said, ‘Pat, is it really 
necessary to take all eight student managers to the Final Four?’ And I said, ‘How many of your 
administrators are going?’ She said, ‘It’ll be fine if you take all eight of your student managers.’

Respect Yourself and Others: Introduction Featuring Teela Jackson 
  
Coach Pat Summitt 
Sometimes I ask myself, ‘Could I play for me?’ The answer is not always yes. 
Back in 1974, I was a 22-year-old head coach, and I had four players who were 21. I was hard on 
them and myself and everyone around me. I thought I had to be. I thought that’s how you 
commanded respect. My first day on the job I stood in front of a packed room of Tennessee 
undergrads who came to open tryouts for the team. I decided I was going to set the tone right 
then and there. I was going to let everyone know how in charge I was.  
I gave a long speech about how demanding it would be to play for the Lady Volunteers. Well, 
after that meeting at least 30 kids never came back. The opening day of practice was pretty 
funny, too. I worked those prospects up and down the court at full speed for two solid hours.  
At the end of that, I ordered them to run a bunch of conditioning drills. I ran them in ‘suicide 
drill’ after ‘suicide drill.’ A group of four young ladies were running together. When they got to 
the end of the line they just kept on running. They ran out the door and up the steps and I 
never saw them again. 
My ideas about how to command respect have changed since then. I’ve learned you can’t 
demand it or whack it out of people with a two-by-four. You have to cultivate it, in yourself and 
those around you. 
  
Teela Jackson, Vice President, HR

Physical Habits for Building Self-Respect from Within

Coach Summitt found that several physical habits help build and sustain self-respect from within:

Keep moving
Meet standing up
Have a brain break each hour
Go exercise
Eat well
Hydrate
Sleep
Remove technology from where you sleep

Select the Start button to learn more about each habit and practical ways to put them into action.


Physical Habit 1

Keep moving

In a world where you can run a business from a computer or mobile device, it’s easy to begin to become sedentary. The world is a better place when we are healthy, so resisting remaining sedentary is good for your long-term physical health.

The habit of Keeping Moving does even more! 

Moving on a consistent basis:

changes your emotional and mental states
enhances blood-flow, and in some cases, moves blood away from your legs and feet and toward the most important destination: your brain

Physical Habit 2

Meet standing up

This may seem a bit awkward at first but give it a try! Many business leaders today begin the morning with a standing, huddle meeting where team members quickly review their priorities for the day and aspirations for outstanding performance.

So why is it helpful to stand up for these meetings? Slouching in your chair, scooting down and getting too comfortable, and perhaps to some degree, relaxing too much, keeps us from feeling active and engaged.

A quick standing meeting that allows you to touch base and review major talking points is both healthy for the body and dynamic for the brain.

Physical Habit 3

Have a brain break each hour

Have you ever found yourself checking social media during your workday? Most of us have. Have you ever found yourself thinking about a movie or music at a time when you’re supposed to be working? Our brains are full of distractions that we must learn to ignore to some degree. 

But part of the reason why we get distracted is because our brains need a bit of a break. In learning, the younger you are, the more breaks you need. In preschool, for example, lessons are made up of very small chunks, giving children playtime in between. The fact is, we aren’t that different than children. 

Although there may be times when this isn’t possible, there are some definite neurological, energy, and creative advantages to taking a break every 60 minutes. Some companies today, for example, actually build in social media update moments throughout the day so that team members can reconnect with their outside world or their social networks. 

Whatever you do with your break, giving yourself five minutes every 60 minutes or so to break your concentration from your work and check in on something you enjoy, setting a timer, and bringing yourself back to your work quickly, can represent a habit that will renew your spirit and ultimately help you to concentrate much better after your break is over.

Physical Habit 4

Go exercise

The best leaders and top performers almost always follow an established exercise routine. They might be training for a marathon, getting a little bit more in shape for an upcoming social event or vacation, or they may have decided that they are going to go for a walk at least four days a week in nature as an expression of gentle exercise. Whatever your workout routine may be, it builds incredible self-confidence and self-respect to consistently follow that routine.  

When you make a commitment to yourself to exercise, and then remain faithful to the commitment to yourself, you not only feel physically better from the exercise, but you also feel emotionally and mentally better about the person you are becoming by staying healthy and keeping your promises.  Also, the time you give up exercising is worth it because invariably the version of you that comes back after exercise will be much more creative and effective.  

When your teammates observe you setting a habit and routine for yourself and living with it, they are far more likely to do the same for themselves and for the work they bring each day.

Physical Habit 5

Eat well

There is no shortage of information on healthy eating today. Although there is a wide variety of purported, healthy diets, what’s important for our purposes is that you do your own research and select eating habits that give you energy and allow you to be your absolute best.  

Most research-based, healthy diets include:

a healthy observation of portion sizes
careful restrictions on fast food
the avoidance of an overindulgence of fat and sugar, and 
the habit of enjoying food that provides nutrients and vitamins to help us stay healthy and replenished.  

Physically, we’ve all seen people that begin to either embrace or depart from the habit of eating well. Leaders who have teams that follow successfully and eagerly recognize that their leader has self-respect, as demonstrated by how they choose to feed themselves.

Physical Habit 6

Hydrate

Doctors will tell you that many of their patients are dehydrated.  Transcendent, high-achieving athletes like NFL quarterback Tom Brady drink more water than most of us can imagine. Water is the ultimate system-cleansing agent and helps with cell rejuvenation. After all, our brains and bodies are made up mostly of water, so consuming our very essence helps keep us energized and rejuvenated.

Physical Habit 7

Sleep

Did you ever hear someone say, “You’d better sleep on it?”  There’s wisdom in letting ideas percolate, giving time for reflection, overnight or otherwise. But there are also numerous other advantages to the habit of sleep.  The great Kobe Bryant was famous for sleeping just a few hours a night, committing even more hours of the day to honing his craft.  Keep in mind, there are individuals who obviously can get by with less sleep, but it’s actually very rare. Most of us can find a way to “get by” with less sleep, but generally speaking, we aren’t at our physical and mental peak when we deny ourselves sleep.  

What would it be like if you were actually rested? How much better could you be?

Physical Habit 8

Remove technology from where you sleep

You may have heard about this old trick for doing well on exams in school. For decades, instructors have suggested that students study for their exam in the place where the exam is going to take place. The reason this is said to work is because when you learn something new, your surroundings and the context of your surroundings make a difference. Learning to hit a tennis ball on a golf driving range wouldn’t create the familiarity in your brain that learning it on a tennis court would.

This idea of familiarity makes a difference to learning something new, and the same is true with technology. If your handheld tools are used on a consistent basis to help you work as hard as you can with the utmost concentration and engagement at work, it’s a bad idea to curl up with that same piece of technology and try to go to sleep. Many people struggle mightily with their sleep habits, due in part to the habit of taking technology to bed with us and our eyes being exposed to the stimulating screen light. Thoughtful leaders once again respect themselves enough to remove these distractions when it benefits them, which in this case results in the restorative advantage of sleep.

Summary

To summarize, there are several important physical habits that can help you establish and build self-respect from within:

Keep moving
Meet standing up
Have a brain break each hour
Go exercise
Eat well
Hydrate
Sleep
Remove technology from where you sleep

## dd_mp_02_st_008 — Mental Habits for Building Self-Respect from Within

Four Mental Habits for Building Self-Respect from Within

In addition to physical approaches to building self-respect, four key mental habits can help establish self-respect from within:

Minimize multitasking
Leverage mornings
Take distraction-free vacations
Turn off notifications

Select the Start button to learn more about each habit and practical ways to put them into action.


Mental Habit 1

Minimize multitasking

Teenagers are famous for telling their parents that they are much better at multitasking than their older teachers or family members. The fact is, multitasking represents a division of your attention, no matter your age. And with every division of your attention, you are less focused and less able to bring the best of yourself to whatever you’re doing. So, to demonstrate self-respect, adopt the habit of minimizing multitasking.  

As a general rule, try to limit multitasking to activities that don’t require a generous amount of brainpower. For example, if you are doing a load of laundry while boiling some eggs and waiting for the dishes in the dishwasher to run a cycle, you may be working in a very efficient manner. 

However, balance that against really important tasks or obligations, such as writing a key report or creating a multimedia presentation for an upcoming meeting. In those instances, the best leaders and peak performers understand that minimizing distractions in those moments and putting full mental power towards the task at hand is a far more powerful strategy for achieving better results. It also helps to demonstrate self-respect and represents an excellent habit for others to follow.

Mental Habit 2

Leverage mornings

There’s no doubt that many of the most successful leaders are "morning people."  They get up, get going, and get more done before work than many get done all day long. Competitive leaders will start making progress on their goals before others even start their day.  

Now, being a morning person sometimes has to do with your body’s natural rhythms, suggesting that if you are not a morning person, you may never be at your best in the early morning hours. The degree to which you can become a morning person is perhaps debatable. 

However, even if you cannot bring it to yourself to get up earlier than others and get a lot of work done before anybody else does, the notion of leveraging mornings has a secondary application.  To leverage mornings can also mean that you follow the old Stephen Covey adage of putting "first things first."  This simple adage suggests that prioritizing the things that matter the most and getting them done before anything else helps foolproof your success and make it more likely that progress towards a goal will happen.  

Whether it’s at 5 a.m. or simply a commitment to schedule the top priorities as early in the day as possible, you’re far more likely to make progress if you put what matters most at times of the day that won’t be interrupted.  Why do so many successful leaders exercise so early in the morning? Often times they will suggest to you that this is a time when interruptions are less likely to occur, allowing them to tackle their health before turning themselves over to their work.

Mental Habit 3

Take distraction-free vacations

The video specifically recommends that you take your vacation time and refrain from taking your work with you. It’s often a sign of honor in hyper-competitive work environments to check in on work during vacation, responding to copious emails and checking in on key performance indicators.  This is not necessarily a sign of strength or self-respect.   

By always checking in, there is an implied suggestion that the system can’t work for a while without you, and that’s not a good sign if you’re a leader.  Checking in during vacations also deviates from your commitment to taking care of yourself and giving yourself the gift of rest.

Great leaders and top performers often take vacations that are more engaging and rigorous than their work. They may trek a challenging landscape or engage in some interesting and rigorous retreat. While a few emails may take longer to respond to, there is a recognition of self-care and the expansion of personal interests that only serve to make a leader or top performer more valuable to the system and the team.  Refraining from staying attached to laptops and other devices goes back to our recommendation about technology as well. Taking a sabbatical from technology for a week definitely has physical, emotional, and spiritual advantages.

Mental Habit 4

Turn off notifications

Notifications are good for business—the app business, that is.  Remember, as wonderful as our phones and mobile devices have become in helping us execute our daily functions, there is embedded functionality within them designed primarily to advance the business interests within the applications on our phones. It is in the best interest of our most popular social media destinations to keep our eyes glued to the screen and to continue scrolling. The same is true for all of our embedded news channels or personal interest apps. Think of each of them like baby birds in the nest, screeching for the mother’s attention. Every time one of these baby bird applications chirps and your phone causes you to move away from whatever you’re doing and towards the content, you are submitting to their business intention instead of your own.  

To be clear, there’s nothing wrong with a good product trying to entice us and capture our attention. However, what we would suggest is that you at least turn off your notifications and refrain from responding like Pavlov’s dog every time one of your favorite app destinations pushes out an update. Simply put, our brain can’t withstand that many interruptions a day. We’re more likely to be successful if we can schedule our own time to catch up on our favorite social media. We get to limit the exposure and choose to spend time in these spaces at a point where it makes sense in our day - not when the app company decides to focus and get our attention.

Summary

To summarize, there are four important mental habits that can help you establish and build self-respect from within:

Minimize multitasking
Leverage mornings
Take distraction-free vacations
Turn off notifications

Emotional Habit 1

Establish weekly and daily gratitude practice

We all can visualize a life and levels of performance that are better than our current situation. More importantly, there are others out there who could only dream of having what you have. To that end, one of the most empowering emotional habits you can establish is to specifically and directly recognize the people, things, achievements, situations, etc. for which you should be grateful.

Writing down what you should be grateful for each week or even daily can give you a deep sense of fulfillment and happiness. It also helps you in striving to achieve more so that our brains begin to see that pleasant things come from hard work and discipline. There’s nothing worse than, for example, working hard towards a goal, achieving that goal, and then not taking the time to be grateful for your achievement!

Emotional Habit 2

Unleash laughter

Throughout history, jesters and comedians have been in high demand.  Funny people tend to remain surrounded by friends and find themselves included in a variety of social situations, often with more invitations than time to fulfill them. This is true because laughter is an amazingly euphoric emotional state. When we laugh, we breathe, we are distracted from our troubles or our daily grind, and we land in a lighter, happier place, even for just a moment. 

This is so important for leaders and high performers, especially in high-stress situations. Leaders who are incredibly competitive can, and arguably should, remain very demanding of themselves and others around them. While this culture of demand may be necessary overall, it can create an ongoing level of stress that requires a break, and that break can come from laughter. Therefore, as a leader or top performer, be willing to laugh at yourself, your circumstance, a situation, or just a crazy moment in the day. Be willing to laugh uproariously, sharing joy with others.  

Psychologists and sociologists have suggested that sharing a laugh helps bring groups closer, which is why you see humor, even in small doses, being used all the time by successful teams. This use of humor does not suggest that everything is a laughing matter, or that your system needs a steady diet of slapstick jokes. Instead, make room for laughter that can break the tension, give yourself an opportunity to connect, and then get right back to the work at hand with renewed focus and joy.

Emotional Habit 3

Declare appreciation

When the people you work with feel good, it’s palpable to others. The leader who genuinely and authentically declares appreciation for coworkers creates an amazing feeling of goodwill on the team or individual. Think about the last time you looked into someone’s eyes and thanked them and told them how much you appreciated them for who they were or what they were doing.

How did you feel the last time someone said that to you? When that appreciation is declared, it’s impossible to ignore, and we share a mutually good feeling. When you declare appreciation for somebody in a group, everyone feels that good grace, and the team invariably grows closer and improves their bond to one another.

Two Truths about Team Respect and Accountability

Here are two important truths to keep in mind:

TRUTH #1: 
"We go is more important than ego." 

                                                   Pat Summitt




How many times have you been on a team when one or more members of the team began working toward their own advantage vs. what’s good for the team? This is the chasm between we go (the team) and ego (the individual). 

When teams are fully formed, they realize that the team is better when all members consistently focus on what’s good for their collective purpose instead of focusing on individual opportunities. In most cases, individual performance can be dramatically enhanced when the team is there to provide support. 

In other words, when teams are fully formed and working effectively, the individual or ego doesn’t have to suffer unnecessarily for the sake of the team because the team, while working together, improves the team outcome and sets the best environment for individual performance.

TRUTH #2: 
Team (peer-to-peer) accountability has more impact than power-based accountability.







With power-based accountability, individuals might be motivated to perform or react due to the expectations of a particular person in some level of authority. This level of influence isn’t an earned area. It is assigned. It means you pay attention or respond because of the authority that exists. A certain amount of authority will always exist. However, the best organizations and the most successful teams respond to leadership because they genuinely respect the leader. 

When the leader creates an expectation of accountability, there’s a desire to reach the expectation out of respect for the leader. To take it one step further, the very best teams don’t wait for the leader’s accountability to kick in. They create their own by supporting and holding each other accountable on the team. This is always the most powerful form of accountability because it’s immediate and ever present. 

Work cultures that are creative, positive, and engaging can’t remain that way if one leader alone carries that expectation. There are frankly too many moments when the boss isn’t there. This has to be an accountability where everybody engages without being forced.

be used against us at a later time.

Behaviors to Avoid

START 

Behavior 1

Gossiping

If you have to look over your shoulder when telling someone on your team something, maybe you shouldn’t say it. Gossip almost always serves to diminish someone in the organization or on the team and ultimately hurts the team as a whole.

12345

Behavior 2

Hoarding Information

Information is power, in almost every professional setting, on every team. Once again, this needs to be addressed and directly discouraged.

12345

Behavior 3

Being a Blame-Thrower

Blame-throwers refuse any level of accountability and are always looking for opportunities to throw other people under the bus for lack of progress or failure. Rather than honestly acknowledging what’s going on with the situation, energy is spent literally finding blame and taking previous time away from finding solutions.

Creating an environment where the team owns and learns from its mistakes, and where teammates cheer on each other's victories, creates a far better environment.

12345

Being Late

12345

Being Negative

"What’s wrong" is usually easier to discover than "what’s right." Furthermore, if the leader is unusually negative, the team will get the message, and they will begin to pursue and present negativity on a more consistent basis, simply following the expectations of the leader.

Negativity can operate like a magnet, pulling in more negativity every day. Therefore, negative individuals cannot be allowed to flourish without confrontation. An environment where negativity is allowed loses respect and momentum.
