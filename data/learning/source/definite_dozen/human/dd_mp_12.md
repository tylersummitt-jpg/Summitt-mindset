Change Is a Must

Change Is a Must: Introduction, featuring Karen Hilton

Peak Performers Seek Change Constantly
Top leaders and peak performers realize that getting to the top and staying on top both require continuous pursuit of change. Growth cannot happen without change. The pursuit of change sounds really good...until we are challenged with actually thinking, feeling, and acting differently. Without question, humans instinctively tend to resist unfamiliar patterns. In a world where we seek excellence or exceptional results, however, it's important to go against our natural instinct of avoiding change. Rather, we must continuously, relentlessly, and passionately pursue it. The willingness and ability to pursue change is part of the definition of success for high-performing leaders.

Familiarity Breeds Laziness
Peak performers learn to loathe familiarity. If something becomes too familiar or easy, it's important for them to shake it up and make it harder. Peak performers and members of teams that become dynasties will win a championship only to return the next year with a new strategy, improvement, or approach designed to improve on previous performance. As a leader, be on the lookout for familiarity. If team members become too familiar and too comfortable, they won't perform at their very best.

Flexibility Wins, Stubbornness Loses
Often, being a novice at an endeavor brings flexibility to the work. A rising performer with great potential but limited experience may be willing to try a greater variety of techniques to take on the champ. Meanwhile, the champion may be less likely to try something new because, after all, they have already won the gold and know what it's like to stand in the winner's circle. This is why experience alone isn't enough. Experience with flexibility is a wonderfully powerful combination. That champion could potentially climb to even greater heights if they embrace flexibility to go along with all that experience.

If You Don’t Like It, Change It — The Mantra of the Most Successful
In teams and in life, stubbornness results in being stuck — a terrible place to be both personally and professionally. If you're stuck in a job you don't like or a situation that makes you uncomfortable, it can feel suffocating. High-performing leaders understand the need to embrace the mantra of change and to be willing to change virtually everything. If your job isn't what you would like, you can change it. If there are things about yourself that you don't like, take steps to change your life so you reach your personal goals. The willingness to change and the desire to improve are at the heart of what it means to be successful.

Take Action!
When people or teams fail to change, it's often due to "analysis paralysis." A team's members may have studied many different ways to address a problem and begin to feel overwhelmed. When there are too many choices, people tend to refrain from taking action. This is why salespeople commonly limit choices so a buyer can feel less overwhelmed and make a buying decision. An effective leader can make sure team members have choices, while keeping those choices simple. Team members must then be expected to commit to take action. Growth, change, and improvement can't happen without action. Even if team members take action and make the wrong decision, they can adjust — but those discoveries don't happen unless they take action in the first place.

What Are Your Limits? Change Will Tell You
The biographies of peak performers and strong leaders often reveal these individuals weren't entirely sure of their own performance limits. However, committing to change and growth often helps people solve that mystery and find out just how high is "high," or what sort of capabilities lie within. You'll never know if you could have been a great coach, CEO, or renowned performer in whatever venue you seek if you don't pursue a plan for improvement and chase it with relentless grit.

Change Requires Internal Shift First
Thinking of change from Gandhi's perspective makes it quite practical and applicable. In order to inspire change in others, you have to be able to conceive of change within yourself. In other words, from within your heart and mind, do you have the ability to conceptualize change and make the shifts necessary to make it happen? Incredible entrepreneurs who want to reshape the world must first overcome their own doubts and surround themselves with people who believe their work can change the world. Companies made up of leaders and top performers who have this ability to shift and believe in themselves usually have a much better chance of getting the market to believe in them.

From Shy Tomboy to Coaching Legend

Change Is a Must: From Shy Tomboy to Coaching Legend, featuring Steve Olson

Leaders who are committed to taking their teams to the next level must be willing to publicly declare they will take whatever action is required to grow and make necessary improvements. Without question, there is something very powerful to a public declaration. Often, someone needs to make a public declaration to make a significant change in their lives. They say it aloud and to enough people that they feel a sense of accountability to make good on their commitment.

You’ve Changed! Why, Thank You!
In this module, we've made it clear that it's a huge compliment if someone says that you've changed (assuming it's a positive change). If people around you notice that you're always changing, it's likely a sign of continuous growth and improvement.

Remember, Change Does Not Always Mean Improvement
This is not to suggest that all change relates to improvement. Have you ever known someone who changed in a negative way? Maybe they developed some poor habits or allowed some influencer to negatively change how they live or how they treat other people. Maybe it's an organizational leader who made ineffective changes just for the sake of change. Perhaps a family member fell victim to some sort of addiction that changed their behavior or lifestyle. Maybe a friend or loved one has changed in a way that causes friction or distance in your relationship. Not all change is improvement. When we use the term "change" here, we mean continuous, positive change that helps you become a better person. Your willingness to create positive change is what ultimately leads to success.

Aspire to Become Something New, and Love the New You
The solution here is to continuously aspire to become a better version of who you are. Visualize that better version of you on the horizon and take whatever action you need to make sure you embody all of those wonderful attributes. Fall in love with the "new version of you" that you were trying to create, and then your ongoing pursuit will become a labor of love.

Commit to Growing Professionally, Forever
When it comes to change, you won't always make the right decisions. You may choose to change something that should have been left alone, and you may have to change back. You may change too quickly and not embrace it for the long haul. Perhaps the most empowering thing you can do is to commit to growing, both personally and professionally, for the rest of your life. If you can always commit to learning and becoming more, there's nothing that you can't achieve. There is no shelf life on learning either. We all know there is no profession today that will allow anyone to sit idly for long without being left behind. Commit to lifelong growth and love the journey.

Change Requires Reaching Beyond the Comfort Zone

Suppose you are attempting to build muscle, but you only lift light, easy-to-move weights. Your body most likely will not respond to this comfortable — if not boring — workout routine. Why We Seek to Avoid Disequilibrium

Equilibrium is the status quo. It's what we are used to — normal habits and patterns that allow us to pretty much keep things the same. When we challenge ourselves and add new levels of intensity to what we do, it creates a sense of disequilibrium. Although it isn't necessarily described this way in personal training, your muscles go through a state of disequilibrium when you push them with heavy weights. Once your body recovers and you have a new level of strength, equilibrium is restored. When you've reached this new level of equilibrium and you challenge yourself with even heavier weights, the disequilibrium emerges again.

Continue to Visualize Who You Want to Become

One of the most famous movie lines of all time is from the Kevin Costner film, "Field of Dreams."

"If you build it, he will come."

From the film, Field of Dreams
Photo Credit: H. Michael Miley, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

In the movie, the narrator whispers the line, "If you build it, he will come" to the main character about taking what appeared to be an absurd risk. A voice in his head told him to build a baseball field on his farm, and it made no sense to anyone except him. Costner's character had a vision for what he wanted to build and, even though there was resistance all around him, he had to commit to that vision to persist. As a leader, if you dream and visualize what you want for yourself, you position yourself to achieve the unachievable or become what you have not yet become. See it first in your mind and heart, and then chase it with your hands, feet, and energy. That is the formula for outstanding success.

Change Makes Us All Uncomfortable — Get Comfortable with Discomfort

Your success as a leader or peak performer may come down to the degree to which you consistently get comfortable with discomfort. Returning to the example of exercise, if someone wants to remain extraordinarily fit for the rest of their life, they must consistently take actions that would be uncomfortable to some people. They may have to make uncomfortable food choices and do uncomfortable physical activities. Getting comfortable with these consistent, yet uncomfortable habits will very likely determine success.

It's also easy for a strong leader or peak performer to get overly comfortable with their own approaches. After all, they've already won and can reference the steps they took to get there. It's uncomfortable to make a winning coach, CEO, or peak performer change their practices to achieve the next level. How many times have championship athletes won the crown, lost a crown, and somewhat reinvented themselves to get the crown back again? Change, then, is at the heart of what it means to be a champion.

Leadership Reflections: Are You Willing to Get Uncomfortable?

Learning yoga requires many hours of "discomfort," trying to achieve proper form and struggling to keep balance. Learning to play an instrument requires many hours of discomfort in the struggle to play fundamental scales and chords. When it comes to trying something new, many of us talk a good game but are rarely willing to actually break through that comfort zone and have a growth opportunity.

Practice breaking out of your comfort zone and identify some areas of growth. What do you need to learn that might require you to start from the very beginning to reach a working level of competence? For example, maybe you could use more proficiency with technology, and it will require asking a lot of questions. Are you too afraid to look like you don't know what you're doing? Think of some areas you'd like to grow and improve.

Reflect on this question in your workbook.

Now that you have identified these areas of weakness, zone in on specifically what you could do to build up your proficiency. Address what it would feel like to grow in these areas. What would you get from this commitment to growth in an area that may be difficult for you currently?
