Lesson 6: Climbing With Confidence  
 
Nikki Anosike 
Raise your hand if you’ve ever felt out of your depth at work? OK, I see you! I see some men’s 
hands up too! Confidence is a crucial asset for success in any professional environment. 
However, for many women, maintaining and bolstering their confidence can be challenging.  
Why? Well, workplace stereotypes that portray women as less competent, assertive, or capable 
of leadership can undermine anyone’s confidence. The underrepresentation of women in senior 
leadership positions can also erode female confidence. Many times, the culprit is simply our 
own perfectionism and a fear of failure.  
That’s why, in this lesson, you’ll learn how to view challenges as opportunities. You see, our 
interpretation of events significantly influences our reactions and decisions. When faced with a 
difficult situation, we have two primary choices: we can see it as a threat or as a chance to learn 
and grow.  
Viewing challenges as opportunities requires a deliberate shift in mindset. It involves 
recognizing that every setback carries the potential for a valuable lesson, a new skill, or a 
chance to build resilience. Failure is often seen as the opposite of success, but in reality, it is a 
key component of the learning process. Each failure highlights areas for improvement and 
making us better. Pat was a big believer in learning from failure. In the business world, this 
concept is known as "failing forward."  
For individuals like you and me, adopting a “fail-forward” mindset can lead to immense personal 
growth. When we stop fearing failure, we become more willing to step out of our comfort zones 
and take on new challenges. This openness to new experiences can lead to unexpected 
opportunities and achievements that we might have otherwise missed. Challenges also test our 
resilience, the ability to bounce back from adversity. 
Instead of thinking, "Why is this happening to me?" ask, "What can I learn from this?" or "How 
can this situation make me stronger?"

## cw_mp_07_st_002 — Have a Growth Mindset

Type: interactive_reading · Source sequence: 2

Lesson 2 of 7
Have a Growth Mindset

Heading

The foundation of a positive attitude also begins with adopting a growth mindset. What do we mean by that? A growth mindset is the belief that abilities or intelligence can be developed through hard work. It’s understanding that our careers aren’t a byproduct of our circumstances or predetermined by external factors, but instead depends on our willingness to be challenged and grow. Some individuals may have to work harder to have a growth mindset in certain circumstances than others, but growth at its core is a resource available to everyone.

Pat firmly believed in a growth mindset, so much so that it was a prerequisite of everyone who played under her leadership. She famously said:
"How can you grow, if you never change? Without changing something almost every year, Tennessee never would have won five national championships…And without accepting some pretty dramatic changes in my own life, I wouldn’t be the head coach of the Lady Vols."

"There is nothing wrong with personal or professional growth, as long as you are strong in who you are and don’t lose sight of where you came from. Self-improvement doesn’t mean abandoning your old identity…Today, if someone tells me I’ve changed, I take it as a compliment. The plain fact is, without change, the game of women’s basketball would never have grown, nor I with it."

So what does a "growth mindset" involve? Click on the icons to expand & view.

Viewing Setbacks as Learning Experiences

As mentioned, setbacks are an inevitable part of any journey, but the key lies in how you respond to them. Instead of dwelling on failures, leaders with a growth mindset see setbacks as valuable experiences. Analyze what went wrong, extract lessons, and use the newfound knowledge to pivot and move forward. You can’t grow if you’re not learning, even if that learning sometimes manifests itself as mistakes.

Listening to Criticism

Constructive criticism is one of the most valuable tools for personal growth. The key is knowing how to seek it out and learn from it. Successful women leaders have mastered both. Instead of seeing feedback as a personal attack, strong women leaders view it as an opportunity to refine their skills. They have learned not to run from or avoid criticism, and know when it has helpful intentions behind it.
“The older I get, the more flexible I’d like to be,” Pat said. “Change is a force of nature; the truth is that nothing, good or bad, lasts forever. There will be setbacks, injuries, and adverse circumstances, and the person who deals best with them will win out.”

Setting Goals Regularly and Adapting

One fundamental part of any career is setting career-related goals. Setting goals is essential for growth, but a growth mindset also requires flexibility when going after those goals. This means regularly examining your objectives and taking into account new information and changing circumstances. Think of it as a game plan that has to be continually re-evaluated at the end of each season. Visualize yourself achieving your career goals and imagine the steps it takes to get there.

Surrounding Yourself With a Growth-Minded Community

It’s no secret that the company we keep plays a huge role in shaping our mindset. Surround yourself with individuals who share a growth mindset and encourage personal development. Engage in positive, growth-minded conversations and avoid relationships built on negativity.

Continuous Learning

The most obvious part of a growth mindset is continuous learning and skill development. The professional landscape is constantly changing, and staying ahead requires continual effort. Seek opportunities to enhance your skills, whether through formal education, workshops, or on-the-job training. Embrace a love of lifelong learning, new technologies, and industry trends. This can include formal education like conferences, workshops or online courses, or informal learning like reading and networking.

Committing to an Attitude of Resilience

Women are historically resilient, and the workplace is no exception. Men and women both have to be resilient at work, but often in different ways. Make it your mission to have an attitude of resilience in your career.  This includes positive self-talk, an attitude of gratefulness, and the willingness to pivot when needed.

## cw_mp_07_st_004 — Confidence and Self-Talk

Lesson 4 of 7
Confidence and Self-Talk

Cultivating Confidence
Confidence is a necessary quality for both men and women in leadership. Yet due to the confidence gap, some women may have to work harder than their male counterparts to develop confidence in their careers. So how do we do that?

Recognize Negative Patterns and Challenge Them

The first step is becoming aware of negative thought patterns. Examples of these include:

Seeing situations in black-and-white terms, without acknowledging the gray areas or nuances.

All-or-Nothing Thinking

Expecting the worst possible outcome, even when evidence suggests otherwise.

Catastrophizing

Drawing broad conclusions based on limited evidence, often leading to negative self-assessment.

Overgeneralization

Taking responsibility for events or outcomes that are beyond your control.

Personalization

Focusing solely on the negative aspects of a situation while ignoring the positive elements.

Filtering

Assuming you know what others are thinking, often attributing negative thoughts to them without evidence.

Assumptions

Setting rigid, unrealistic expectations for oneself and others, leading to constant feelings of inadequacy (e.g., “I should have finished that report sooner!”).

"Should" Statements

Heading

Once you’ve learned how to limit negative thought patterns, the next step is replacing the negative thoughts with positive self-talk.

Become a Master of Self-Talk

Pat practiced self-talk with her players, especially in tight, important games. “Our attitude [was] ‘You couldn’t be better prepared. You know exactly what to do. So go do it,’” she recalls.

The power of self-talk is undeniable, influencing our thoughts, emotions, and ultimately, our actions. One key to being a master of positive self-talk is to practice affirmations regularly.

Daily Affirmations

Affirmations are powerful statements that reinforce positive beliefs about oneself. To do this, create a list of affirmations tailored to your professional goals and repeat them regularly. For example:
I am right where I need to be in this moment.
I have enough time to do everything that is important to me.
I am strong, I am powerful, and I have everything I need.
I greet this day with gratitude.
I am more than up to the challenge.
I am at the peak of my career—and the best is yet to come!

Replace "I Can't" with "I Can"

The language we use internally shapes our mindset and influences our actions. Swap limiting phrases like "I can't" with empowering alternatives like "I can" or "I will."
Instead of: “I can’t master pivot tables.”
Reframe: “I will master pivot tables in Excel!”
Instead of: “I can’t lead a meeting that confidently.”
Reframe: “I can lead a meeting that confidently, with some practice.”

Constructive Self-Criticism vs. Destructive Self-Talk

There's a fine line between constructive self-criticism and destructive self-talk. Instead of dwelling on mistakes with statements like, "I always mess up," or “I should have known better,” reframe your self-talk to focus on improvement: "I can learn from this and do better next time."

Celebrate Your Strengths

Women often downplay their achievements or attribute success to external factors. Shift your self-talk by consciously focusing on your strengths and celebrating your accomplishments. (“I know how to rock a speech and can do it again!”) Acknowledge the unique skills, talents, and value you bring to your team.

Surround Yourself With Positive Influences

Finally, the people we surround ourselves with significantly impact our mindset! Seek out positive influences and build a supportive network for your leadership career. Engage with colleagues, mentors, or friends specifically who uplift and encourage you. Share your goals and aspirations with those who genuinely celebrate your successes. By surrounding yourself with positivity, you create an environment that automatically reinforces constructive self-talk and bolsters your confidence.

Deep Dive: The Three Types of Confidence

When we show up to the present moment with all of our senses, we invite the world to fill us with joy. The pains of the past are behind us. The future has yet to unfold. But the now is full of beauty simply waiting for our attention.

OK, so we all know confidence is important to having success as a leader. But did you know that not all confidence is the same? There are three main types of confidence that you will encounter in your career:

Often characterized as "fake it till you make it,” isn’t all bad – it can be somewhat useful when starting a new challenge or career role. It involves leaning heavily on your past successes to navigate the unfamiliar territory until you can build confidence of your own in that area.

False Confidence

Conditional confidence means confidence that fluctuates based on external outcomes. When things are going well, you’re confident in that area! When things aren’t going well, doubt tends to creep in, and you feel insecure about your abilities. This type of confidence is a trap many people fall into during their careers.

Conditional Confidence

Unconditional confidence is the end goal, where your confidence and overall self-assurance is largely rooted in the things you can control—your habits, effort, and attitude—rather than external factors. This stable, more resilient form of confidence is key to thriving as a leader in your career.

Deep Dive: Self-Talk and Affirmations 
 
Nikki Anosike 
The second bookend to having confidence is learning to use affirmations and confidence 
anchors. Affirmations are positive, specific statements that help individuals overcome 
negative thoughts and self-sabotage.  
The science behind affirmations lies in their ability to influence our subconscious mind. 
When we consistently repeat positive statements, we create new neural pathways that 
strengthen our positive self-beliefs. This process is known as neuroplasticity, where the 
brain adapts and reorganizes itself by forming new connections. In other words, by 
repeating certain affirmations every day, you can actually rewire your brain to think more 
constructively and have more confidence! 
For example, someone struggling with self-esteem issues might use affirmations like, "I am 
worthy of love and respect, " or "I am capable and confident. " The effectiveness of 
affirmations depends on how they are phrased and used. Here are some key principles to 
consider: 
Be Positive. Fame affirmations in a positive manner. Instead of saying, "I am not a failure, " 
say, "I am successful in almost everything I do. ” 
Be Present. Use the present tense to create a sense of immediacy. Affirmations should 
reflect the present moment, such as, "I am strong and resilient. " 
Be Specific. Tailor affirmations to address specific areas of concern or goals. For instance, 
if you want to boost your public speaking skills, an affirmation could be, "I am working 
every day to being more confident and well- spoken when speaking in public. " 
Be Personal. Make affirmations personal and meaningful to you. They should resonate with 
your values and aspirations, not someone else’s. 
Be Repetitive. Finally, consistency is key! Repeat your affirmations daily, ideally multiple 
times a day, to reinforce the positive messages. 
The goal of an affirmation isn’t to repeat something that is false – for example, if you are 
struggling with a health issue, you wouldn’t say, “I am in perfect health!” You might say 
something like “I am getting better and better every day!” or “I don’t need to feel perfect – I 
have all the energy I need to make this day a great one. ” An affirmation is simply phrased

around the end goal, the part of your mind that you can control, reminding yourself that you 
are making progress. 
For example, when I was a player, I needed to hear I wasn’t alone, so I would always repeat to 
myself “You’re not alone. You’ve got great coaches and teammates that are working towards the 
goal with you.”  
You also want to take advantage of confidence anchors. Confidence anchors are simply visual 
reminders of confident feelings. Maybe it’s a medal from the last time you ran a half-marathon, 
to remind you can do challenging things. Maybe it’s a coffee mug someone gave you the last 
time you crushed a key project that makes you feel like a boss whenever you look at it! Think 
about a time when you felt exceptionally confident and empowered and select a physical object 
or image that you can associate with the positive experience. 
Confidence – and learning how to actively grow your confidence – is key to anyone climbing 
with success, male or female. So, let’s learn more.
