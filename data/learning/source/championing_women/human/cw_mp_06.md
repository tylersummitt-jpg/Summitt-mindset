The Strength of Female Leadership

Now that we’ve talked about biases and roadblocks for women, let’s talk about their strengths.
While it’s important to avoid automatic biases about either sex, the journey to workplace equality doesn’t mean ignoring some of the unique attributes and characteristics that women typically bring to the workforce.
First, a few ways in which men and women are the same—a myriad of studies show both sexes have similar levels of education, ambition, and drive/work ethic. Yet there are some biological nuances between men and women that employers can use to their advantage when building teams and encouraging female leaders. In other words, being equal doesn’t necessarily mean the same.

## cw_mp_06_st_002 — A Balance to the Force

Type: interactive_reading · Source sequence: 2

Lesson 2 of 5
A Balance to the Force

Heading

Other than diversity (and fairness), let’s look at some specific reasons you should want women on your team.
Each gender has its super-skills—or traits that come more naturally to one gender than the other.

Attention to Detail

On the whole, women remember details easily. Biologically, females have a larger hippocampus, which is where the memory part of the brain is stored, allowing them to bring complex bits of detailed information to mind quickly and readily. This can be an invaluable skill in just about any workplace. Women demand perfection and generally hold their work to high standards.

Emotional Intelligence

Traditionally, women excel in the soft skills, meaning they readily pick up on environment, verbal and body language cues (EQ). They have a natural gift for empathy, intuition, and are in tune with the overall dynamic of the team, all of which are key leadership skills. Their capacity to understand and respond to the emotions of others can lead to improved conflict resolution, reduced workplace stress, and increased employee engagement.

Heightened Intuition in Decision-Making

Studies show men often take a fact-based approach to their environment, categorizing and sorting things based on data. Women lead with intuition and feeling, partially because of their heightened social perceptions around communication and conflict. While men tend to sort things into black and white, women often tend to involve more details in their decision-making processes. Why does this matter? For optimal decision-making, you need both—not just facts, but data plus supporting details and EQ.

More Verbally Communicative

Because men compartmentalize things more readily, they often prefer solitude or low-energy distractions after an especially eventful day. The female brain is more active at rest, which is why women tend to want to process things verbally or socially in times of stress. Women are more likely to speak up in the workplace and not bottle things up—if they feel safe doing so—which can be an invaluable  leadership skill.

Risk-Averse, Multi-tasking and Resiliency

Women are often more risk-averse than men. Their caution is an important balance to workplace decisions and long-term planning.
Women often juggle multiple responsibilities in both their personal and professional lives. In a fast-paced work environment, these skills are invaluable for meeting deadlines, managing projects efficiently, and maintaining a healthy work-life balance. Women are also highly adaptable, a key quality in the face of adversity.

Analytical

Women have also been known to excel in the following areas: multitasking, problem-solving, organization, and planning. They are keenly analytical, but in a different way than men—always analyzing personal interplay, like a computer program running in the background of their mind. Female receptors can sometimes be more heightened to hidden body language or communication nuances, allowing them to synthesize and process large amounts of information.

Collaborative

As team members, women often prove to be highly collaborative. They are also more likely to take on unspoken work that contributes to the team dynamic. This is important because a team that is highly skilled but can’t get along is a poorly functioning team. Women realize this, and have a natural tendency to seek conflict resolution. Women often display less ego, as they have less of the innately wired Alpha-male signaling than their testosterone-bearing counterparts. On the contrary, many women feel the need to qualify or soften their statements to make them more humble, and therefore more socially acceptable:
“I could be wrong, but…”
“I just wanted to ask…”
“This may be a dumb question, but…”

## cw_mp_06_st_004 — A Word on Female Strengths

Type: video_and_reading · Source sequence: 4

Lesson 4 of 5
A Word on Female Strengths

It’s important to note that while there are certain attributes or characteristic patterns that lend themselves more readily to one gender, blatant stereotyping eliminates the uniqueness of each individual and the potential contributions. Just as it would be incorrect to assume all men want to dress up in suits and charge corporate America or spend time in the wild wearing camouflage and a shotgun, it would be incorrect to assume that all women have a higher EQ or are better at multitasking than a man in the next cubicle.

This simply isn’t true. Humans are multi-faceted, and the extent to which an individual displays certain characteristics might lie just as equally with personal development as it does with psychological wiring. Summitt herself, known for skirts and heels on the sidelines, could fix a tractor, mow hay, plow a field, “fire a barn,” and call cows, in addition to being a devoted mother and coaching championship-level NCAA teams.

While experts agree women display patterns of high competence in these areas, they aren’t the only skills women bring to the table. It should be noted that in addition to the characteristics already mentioned, women have been proven to display an equal level of general leadership characteristics to men. For example, according to one study from the Harvard Business Review, which analyzed thousands of 360-degree reviews, women outscored men on 17 of the 19 capabilities that differentiate excellent leaders from average, such as initiative, self-development, speed, being results-driven, integrity, mentorship, motivation, and bold leadership.1

Heading

Therefore, while it’s definitely worth taking note of natural gifts, we don’t prescribe turning these into biases toward females or males in the workplace, as this can cause harm.

Deep Dive: Having Diversity on Your Team

In today’s rapidly evolving business landscape, the importance of diversity cannot be overstated. Among the various dimensions of diversity, women in leadership stand out as an indispensable part of driving innovation, improving decision-making, and creating a more inclusive culture for everyone.
Simply put, the female perspective represents half of society. This is invaluable in many different fields: marketing, product research, advertising, human resources, finances, customer service, etc. You wouldn’t want only the male perspective marketing to the general population, treating patients, or developing groundbreaking research any more than you would want a female only perspective.
