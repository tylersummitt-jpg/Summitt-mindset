Morgan Vance 
The King, The Iron Lady, The People's Princess, The Notorious B.I.G., The Baltimore Bullet, The 
Queen of Country Music. Chances are, each of these statements brought a unique individual to 
mind, and these names were given for a reason: Elvis Presley, Margaret Thatcher, Princess 
Diana, Christopher Wallace, Michael Phelps, and Dolly Parton. 
These people were each known for a certain set of attributes, including having a very, very 
strong personal brand. You may not be a celebrity, pop star, or talk show host, but you yourself 
do have a personal brand. We all do. And while we can't control a lot of the things that go on 
around us, no one can control your personal brand but you. You are responsible for the 
attitude, image, and skill set that comes to mind when people think of you. 
Have you ever thought about it before? Maybe, maybe not, but it's real, whether we choose to 
think about it or not. So what do you want people to think about when your name comes to 
mind? What do they currently think of? 
When I was growing up, I knew I wanted to be a leader and make a difference in my 
community, but I also knew I wanted to be a mom. Growing up in Knoxville, I received the 
benefit of watching strong female leaders like Pat build their personal brands. She was a leader 
both on and off the court and showed me that you could be successful in both your 
professional and personal life. 
I will never forget watching her come to high school basketball games and sit silently on the 
front row, rooting on her son. She didn't draw attention to herself. She didn't let her 
professional work come into her personal time. She was there to be a mom. 
The truth is, personal brand matters and you can shape yours. Let's start by defining what a 
personal brand is. Your personal brand is a unique combination of your skills, experiences, 
values, and personality that sets you apart from others. It's your identity, how you present 
yourself to the world, both online and offline. 
Whether it's through your interactions with colleagues, social media, or the skills you provide as 
part of your daily routine, your personal brand is always on display. Personal brand is the key to 
differentiating yourself from others with similar skills or qualifications. By showcasing your 
unique strengths, passions, and values, you position yourself as a distinct and memorable 
individual. 
An authentic personal brand is one that aligns with your true self. Trying to be someone you're 
not can be exhausting and unsustainable in the long run. At its core, a positive personal brand is

built on attitude and trustworthiness. When you consistently deliver on your promises and 
showcase your expertise, you establish credibility in your field. 
A strong personal brand can leave a lasting positive impression, opening doors to new 
opportunities. So take a minute and think about where you are with your personal brand. What 
things do you like? What don't you like? And this lesson will explore the concept of personal 
brands some more, as well as exercises you can do to uncover yours.

## potl_mp_05_st_004 — The Queen of Rocky Top

Type: reading · Source sequence: 4

Lesson 4 of 10
The Queen of Rocky Top

Heading

The Legend. The General. The Queen of Rocky Top. The Torchbearer. The Godmother of Women’s Basketball.
Pat herself had a strong personal brand built around her dedication to the sport, her unwavering work ethic, and commitment to excellence both on and off the court. She was known for her toughness, her ability to motivate and inspire her players, and her strong leadership skills. She was also known for her composure, the “Summitt Stare,” and her well-manicured look on the sidelines.

Believe it or not, Pat’s own personal brand went through quite a transformation in a period of just a few short years. Raised on a farm in Henrietta, Tenn., in high school she was known as Trish (short for Patricia) Head. When she went to college, her sorority friends assumed she was Pat, and she didn’t correct them.
“Trish Head was a farm kid with crooked teeth and all the wrong clothes who said ain’t, reckon, and yonder,” Pat recalled. “You never get a second chance to make a first impression. In those early days at college, I realized that presentation counts. For better or worse, strangers made sweeping judgements based on my appearance and demeanor. So, I began the process of self-transformation.”1

She quickly enlisted two friends to help her with her grammar and accent, and she enrolled in speech classes. “I began to pay attention to my clothes,” she said. “Dressing appropriately for a role can help you play it better.” She began to wear tailored suits on the sidelines and got braces when she was nearly 30.
In a few short years, gone was the shy farm kid who always wore jeans and was self-conscious about her height. Summitt’s players affectionately referred to her as “Sarge” for her command and polish on the sidelines. Others called her “The Iron Lady” and marveled at her ability to consistently appear in a polished wardrobe reflecting the University of Tennessee’s signature colors. She even became known for a particular shade of blue: “Summitt Blue,” now recognized and worn by the University of Tennessee in athletic uniforms.

Lesson 5 of 10
Why Personal Brand Is Important

Heading

As someone who spent their life in the spotlight, Summitt’s personal brand was well-known. Your own brand may be less distinct, but it exists all the same. We’ll say it again: Your personal brand exists, whether you recognize that it does or not!

Scroll through to complete this element.

What Is Personal Brand?

Personal brand incorporates a lot of things, but without overcomplicating it, there are four main parts:
●      Attitude
●      Image
●      Skills
●      Experience

Conclusion

When considering what your personal brand is, consider what you are known for specifically in each of these four main areas.

Attitude

What is the attitude you present in your role? What principles do you stand for? How do you communicate?

Image

What words would others use to describe you? What is your own signature look? What are you known for?

Skills

What expertise can you be counted on for? What things do you bring to the table? What is your superpower or special skill in your role?

Experience

Finally, what experiences make you unique? What events in your background are you known for that make up part of who you are?

## potl_mp_05_st_007 — Does it Matter?

Type: reading · Source sequence: 7

Lesson 7 of 10
Does it Matter?

Does Personal Brand Matter if the Output Is Good?

You may ask, “Does personal brand at work really matter if my output is good?” In a word, yes. In fact, it matters a whole lot more than you may think.

Personal brand affects consideration for promotions, specific tasks you’re given, what information you can be trusted with, whether you’re asked to present in a meeting, and whether your teammates want to work with you and follow you. After all, no one wants to work with (or follow) a successful jerk.

Your personal brand can have a profound impact on your personal life too, including your relationships and your overall success. Personal brand can sometimes even make up for a direct lack of skill or experience in certain areas.

Lesson 4: Getting a Speeding Ticket with Pat 
Jimmy Delaney 
Pat notoriously was late. She would never tell you this, but she was always late. And she was 
always running behind and always kind of doing makeup in the car and all of that. 
And so anytime that was happening, if I was with her, I would always say, "Let me drive and you 
go in the passenger seat." And we were late. And she told me to get there as quick as I could 
because she didn't want to be late and didn't want that kind of image on her or whatever it 
was. 
And so, we were driving and I was going well above the speed limit. And next thing I know, the 
red and blue lights are behind us. And I was like, "Pat, we're getting pulled over." And she's like, 
"All right, we'll pull over." 
And so, we pull over and I knew I was doing almost double the speed limit. And I was like, "This 
is going to be really, really bad." And I'm starting to dig out my ID and all of this kind of stuff. 
And the officer was walking up with a very angry look on his face because he was pulling over a 
very nice car that was going way too fast. 
And I rolled down the window and before I could say a word, Pat from the passenger seat leans 
over against me and almost kind of like pushes me back into the seat. She's like, "Officer, I'm 
sorry. It's all my fault. I'm late. And I just, you know, we were trying to get there as quick as we 
could." 
And the officer never made eye contact with me, straight to Pat. And he's like, "Now coach, you 
know you're not supposed to be driving that fast," as if I was like a ghost driving the car or 
whatever it was. 
She was like, "I know, I'm so sorry. We're just trying to get to West Knoxville for this thing. I 
promise I won't do it again." 
And he's like, "All right, coach. Good to see you. Good luck this season." 
And so, I'm sitting there like, "What in the world just happened here?" And I roll the window 
back up and I look at her, and she goes back and she goes, "Hit it." And we just took off.
