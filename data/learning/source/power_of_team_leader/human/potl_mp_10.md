Lesson 9: You Have to Demonstrate Loyalty to Receive It 
Joan Cronan 
In today's fast-paced world, businesses face fierce competition and constant challenges. And to 
thrive in such an environment, employees need to be dedicated and committed employees 
who share their vision and their values. Loyalty from employees translates into increased 
productivity, reduced turnover, and a competitive work culture. When employees feel valued 
and supported, they are more motivated to invest their skills and contribute meaningfully to 
the organization's success. They are also more likely to go the extra mile to be passionate 
ambassadors for the company. 
But despite its significance, loyalty seems to be a diminishing principle in today's workplace. In 
the pursuit of profit and growth, some organizations prioritize short-term gains over long-term 
relationships with employees. Downsizing, outsourcing, and frequent restructuring can leave 
employees feeling dispensable and undervalued. Similarly, employees may view employers 
primarily as steppingstones to advance their careers, leading to a lack of commitment and 
loyalty. 
So, how can we cultivate loyalty in the workplace? It begins with a two-way street of 
appreciation between employees and employers. Nurturing a loyal workforce requires creating 
a culture that values people over profit. Encouraging work-life balance, promoting employees' 
well-being, and offering flexibility are essential aspects of this. Employers can also demonstrate 
loyalty by providing: 
• Competitive compensation 
• Benefits 
• Opportunities for growth and development 
On the other hand, employees can exhibit loyalty by: 
• Maintaining a positive attitude 
• Operating with integrity 
• Avoiding gossip 
• Giving 100% on the job 
No matter what product or service you're offering, an organization is only as good as its people. 
Everything begins and ends with the people you have on your staff. Coach Summit knew this 
and prioritized loyalty highly among her favorite Definite Dozen leadership principles. You 
know, when I think of Pat and how she worked with her staff, it was incredible. Never, ever did 
she just assume they were there to help her. She always assumed that they were to work

together. You know, one time we won a championship and I was so excited that when we came 
back for the celebration, I was able to get a Mercedes for Pat to drive for a year for winning a 
championship. So we presented it at the celebration and everybody was thrilled. Well, the next 
year when Pat and I were visiting about if we might win a championship or when we would win 
a championship, she said, let's do something for our assistants. So I got a Mercedes for all three 
assistants because all of them had been there for the three previous championships. It was so 
much fun to watch Pat enjoy her assistants get excited. 
In short, loyalty in the workplace is not just an abstract concept, but a tangible force that 
influences an organization's success. By cultivating loyalty to employers and employees alike, 
we create an ecosystem of dedication that propels our businesses to new levels. Loyalty is not 
just about staying at an organization, but about profound commitment to thrive together.

## potl_mp_10_st_003 — Employee Turnover

Type: reading · Source sequence: 3

Lesson 3 of 6
Employee Turnover

Heading

The recent increase in employee turnover has led to a countering philosophy on the end of employers: Everyone is expendable. With full-time HR departments packed with recruiters, filling seats to replace empty ones has become a booming industry in itself. A new culture has emerged on behalf of the organization: Hire all the top talent you can find, and let nature take its course. We’ll do our best, but if people don’t like it, they can leave, and we’ll replace them with another highly paid person. No one’s really expecting team members to have loyalty, and the organization or company is not showing loyalty in return.

The kicker is… no one really likes this arrangement. Not the employee, not the employer. No matter how practical the hiring industry becomes, if you’re an employee, it can be exhausting to look for jobs every four years. If you’re an employer, it can cost up to 33 percent of an employee’s salary to replace the ones who leave ($15,000 for a $45,000 full-time position).2 Not to mention the impact it makes on an organization’s culture and employees’ sense of well-being.

On their end, employees have watched employers over-hire thousands of employees, then layoff thousands at a time to balance things out when the market corrects. Employees who do survive the layoffs often end up watching their colleagues being axed through emails with little notice, and are then asked to plod on, showing unquestioning loyalty to the company. Employers, by contrast, have scrambled to implement the best benefits programs and most flexible work arrangements available, only to watch their employee base hemorrhage through mass exoduses.

If you want to develop loyalty, the first thing you have to do is demonstrate it. Too many people use the term loyalty unilaterally. Especially bosses. They demand loyalty without reciprocating. They ask ‘Staff, have you been loyal to me?’ But what about the manager’s loyalty to the staff?”

Lesson 9: Pat Sings Rocky Top 
Jimmy Delaney 
When Pat was coaching, it was mid to late 2000s, and the men's basketball coach that was 
here, you know, kind of did some crazy antics and all of that, and painted his chest and came to 
a game. And national news and great support and the fans loved it and all of that. 
And so, Pat was sitting there and she was like, well, I need to do something for a men's 
basketball game. And what is it that I can do? And I wanted to be kind of something wild and 
fun and crazy. And so we were sitting around the table, Dean Lockwood and Holly and Nikki and 
a few of us were all just trying to throw out ideas and this and that. 
And what a lot of folks may or may not realize, Pat, you know, she never said she had the 
greatest singing voice, but she loved Rocky Top and she loved singing Rocky Top. And so 
whether, you know, she was having a dinner at her house or, you know, speaking engagement, 
whatever it was, at some point she kind of would work in breaking into Rocky Top. 
And so we're like, Pat, what if you go out and you sing Rocky Top? And she's like, well, I can do 
that, but is that enough? Like he painted his chest. And so we started talking more and we're 
like, well, what if we kind of did it under the slight of hand and, you know, we have all the 
cheerleaders kind of crowd around you and you just hear this voice. And it might take people a 
second to recognize the voice or whatever it is, but you're kind of singing and all of that. 
And then we're like, what if you guys dressed up as cheerleaders? And there was about 30 
seconds of silence in the room. And I was like, okay, so that's the bad idea and I'm never going 
to be invited to a brainstorm again. And she looked at me and she goes, you can get us 
cheerleader uniforms that'll fit all of us? And I was like, sure we can. 
And she was like, all right, so I run off, get with the cheer coach, get the uniforms, bring them 
back in, lay them on the table a day or two later. And everyone comes in and Pat's like, all right, 
we're doing this. And there was about five to 10 minutes of laughter because I think at that 
point in their career and their life, they had never imagined that they'd be wearing a 
cheerleading uniform. Dean Lockwood probably was my favorite because the minute he put on 
the top and the pants, he immediately started going into the arm pumping and all of that. 
But we got the entire coaching staff, Pat included, dressed up in cheerleading outfits and 
nobody knew about it. Got them down into the tunnel at the men's game. Peyton Manning 
happened to be at that game, which was kind of even more incredible. Dick Vitale was calling 
the game for TV. And so everything was lining up, a sold-out game and all of that.

just going to be a bit where the cheerleaders come out. And we're standing back there. 
And I remember Pat said, she goes, “How do I look?” And I said, “Ridiculous, but you go on in 30 
seconds.” And so, she grabbed the mic and she said, “Is this thing on?” I said, “Oh, it's on.” And 
so the timeout happens, and the cheerleaders crowd around her, and she starts into the “Wish 
that I was...” and the place immediately goes nuts. And I don't know that I've heard Thompson 
Bowling Arena louder. And then they make a pyramid, and they put Pat at the top. And that 
was one of like the lasting images I have.
